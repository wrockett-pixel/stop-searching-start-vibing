    The what

    What is vibe coding?

    Describe the app you want in plain English. AI builds it. You test it and ask for changes.

    You describe itAI writes the codeYou test and improve
    More info
    • No coding skills needed. The AI writes the code.
    • Real web apps. Students click, type and get feedback.
    • You're the expert. You bring the standard, the content and the answer key, and you check that it's right.
    • The term was coined by AI researcher Andrej Karpathy in 2025.

    See the prompt

    Attach your handout, then paste this.

    Turn my attached handout into a one-page interactive web app for my AP World History students.

Keep my questions, readings, videos and order exactly. Before you build, ask me for my answer key. Don't invent answers.

- Show one stop at a time with a progress bar.
- Put each video or reading right beside its questions.
- Where a question has one right answer, students must get it right to move on. Give a hint, never the answer.
- Keep the reaction and SAQ questions as typed responses.
- End with a "Copy my results" button students can paste into the assignment.
- Use dyslexia-friendly text and read-aloud, and make it work on Chromebooks and phones.

    Students do, not just read

    Instant feedback

    Supports for every learner

    Harder to hand off to AI

    More info
    • Shrinking budgets: less money for premium software.
    • AI fatigue: chatbots hand students more text to read. Apps give them something to do.
    • Combats cheating: students work through every step themselves, so they're less likely to use AI to do the work for them.
    • The tools caught up: free AI can build a working app in minutes, and many districts already give teachers Gemini, Copilot or ChatGPT.

    We do The recipe

    From egg to apex in 6 steps

    Plan
    1. Choose your dig siteOne activity to improve
    2. Pack your field kitTopic, standard and grade
    Hatch
    1. Hatch itType your prompt, get a first draft
    2. Evolve itTest like a student, one change at a time
    Release
    1. Share itA link students can open
    2. Save your recipeReuse your prompt next unit
    Print all 6 steps (PDF) ↓
    More info

    1 · Choose your dig site

    One problem, one small app: a worksheet students rush through, a review that falls flat, or a concept they always mix up.

    2 · Pack your field kit

    Your topic, standard and goal; your vocab and questions (or paste in the handout); grade, reading level, devices and accessibility needs.

    3 · Hatch it

    Type your prompt and let the AI build a first draft. Don't expect perfect.

    4 · Evolve it

    Play it like a student and check every fact. Ask for one change at a time, like "Add a hint when they get it wrong." If something breaks, describe what you see.

    5 · Share it

    Google Apps Script, Netlify, or your LMS. Each gives students a plain link with no AI account needed. See Share it with students.

    6 · Save your recipe

    Keep your best prompt as a template, put it in a Claude Project, or make it a Claude skill.

    Make it a Claude skillA skill teaches Claude your recipe once. After that, Claude uses it on its own whenever you ask for that kind of app. Our One Prompt, Every Learner setup is one.
    1. Start a new chat and say: I want to create a skill for building review games for my class. Attach your best prompt and an app you liked.
    2. Answer Claude's questions about how you build: your theme, levels, hints and accessibility rules.
    3. Claude packages it as a skill. Save it, then check it's turned on in Settings › Capabilities › Skills.
    4. Next time, just ask: Make a review game for Unit 4. If something's off, ask Claude to update the skill.

      Step-by-step instructions: Apps Script into Canvas
      
      
      Transforming AI Artifacts into Live Canvas Assets
      
      
      Guide by Taylor Stevens, Ed.S. · Instructional Designer, Forsyth Virtual Academy
      

        
      
      1. 1
        Prepare your code from the AI
        
        
        • In your AI chat, find the code for the interactive tool.
        • Click the Copy icon.
        • Make sure you have the full code. It usually starts with <!DOCTYPE html> and ends with </html>.
        2. 
      
      2. 2
        Create an Apps Script project
        
        
        • Go to script.google.com.
        • Click + New project in the top left.
        • Rename the project at the top, for example "WWI Home Front Simulation."
        3. 
      
      3. 3
        Set up the backend (Code.gs)
        
        
        Every Google web app needs a small "server" instruction that tells Google how to show the file. In Code.gs, delete the existing code and paste this exactly:
        
        
        function doGet() {
  return HtmlService.createHtmlOutputFromFile('index')
         .setXFrameOptionsMode(HtmlService.XFrameOptionsMode.ALLOWALL);
}
        
        
        Why this matters: the .setXFrameOptionsMode line is the "magic" command that lets your app be embedded in an iframe in Canvas. Without it, the browser blocks the connection.
        4. 
      
      4. 4
        Set up the frontend (index.html)
        
        
        • Click the + next to Files in the left sidebar and choose HTML.
        • Name the file index. Google adds .html for you.
        • Delete the template code and paste the entire code you copied from the AI.
        • Save your work: Cmd+S or Ctrl+S.
        5. 
      
      5. 5
        Deploy the web app
        
        
        • Click the blue Deploy button at the top right and choose New deployment.
        • Click the gear icon next to "Select type" and choose Web app.
        • Description: for example, "Initial launch."
        • Execute as: Me (your email).
        • Who has access: Anyone. This is critical, so students can open it without logging into the script editor.
        • Click Deploy and copy the Web app URL. It ends in /exec.
        6. 
      
      6. 6
        Embed it in Canvas
        
        
        • Open your Canvas page or assignment.
        • Switch to the HTML editor (the </> icon).
        • Paste this code, replacing the URL with your link from step 5:
        
        
        <iframe src="YOUR_WEB_APP_URL_HERE" width="100%" height="700px" style="border:none;"></iframe>
        
        
        Save the page. Your interactive asset is now live.
        7. 
    
      
    
      Important tips for teachers
      
    
      
      
      The "New Version" ruleSaving your script doesn't change what students see. Go to Deploy › Manage deployments, edit your active deployment, and choose New version to push updates live. The link stays the same.
      
      
      Test it like a studentOpen the link in a private or Incognito window. If it asks for a sign-in, check that Who has access is set to Anyone. If you're signed into several Google accounts, Apps Script can show "page not found," so test in a private window.
      
      
      SecurityThese apps are sandboxed by Google. They can't access your personal files or the student's computer unless you specifically write code to do so.
      
      
      PersistenceApps hosted on your Google account stay active as long as the account exists, with no third-party hosting fees. Apps on a school account go away if that account is closed, so keep a copy of your code.

      One Prompt, Every Learner

      More info
      
      
      Differentiated
      3 warm-ups recommend a level. The student chooses: Support, On-Level or Challenge.
      Same content and number of questions. Only wording and scaffolds change. Tiered hints, vocab pop-ups, retry with a new version.
      
      
      Vocab help
      Every vocab word is a button. Tap it to see a student-friendly definition in a pop-up, with read-aloud.
      Students get help in the moment without leaving the activity, at every level.
      
      
      Dyslexia-friendly
      Clear font, 18px+, extra spacing, soft backgrounds. Read-aloud with word highlighting.
      Reading settings any student can open. Spelling isn't penalized. Voice typing allowed.
      
      
      Academic integrity
      Students are told up front what's tracked. Copying questions and pasting answers is blocked, so it's harder to hand the work to AI.
      Leaving the page is counted and timed. Inactivity check-in: "Why were you away?"
      
      
      Clear reports
      Student: score, time on task, times away.
      Teacher: results go automatically to a Google Sheet, most-flagged first.
      
        
      Red · away 5+ timesYellow · away 1–4 timesBlue · accommodation
      
    
      
    
      Same rigor at every level. Flags are signals, not proof. Use them to start a conversation. No student names are shared with AI; they go only to your Sheet.
      
  
      

      


      
  
      
    
      
    
      
      
      If we have time · field notes
      
      
      Tips & tricks
      
      
      
        
      Use the Copy button to grab the codeDon't highlight the code by hand.
      When the AI finishes your app, you'll need its code to share it, for example to paste into Google Apps Script. Look for the Copy button on the code window: in Claude it's at the top of the code view, and in Gemini and ChatGPT it's at the top of the Canvas. One click copies every line. Dragging to highlight by hand often misses a few characters at the start or end, and one missing line can break the whole app.
      
        
      Apps won't save students' workAdd a "Copy my results" button or a screenshot screen.
      These apps don't save anything a student does. When students close or refresh the page, their answers and progress are gone. Ask the AI to add a "Copy my results" button so students can paste their answers into the assignment, end with a "screenshot your result" screen, or send results to a Google Form or Sheet.
      
        
      One change at a timeTest after every change.
      Asking for five fixes at once often breaks something that already worked, and then it's hard to tell which change caused it. Ask for one change, test it, then ask for the next.
      
        
      Check every factPlay it before students do.
      AI can get content wrong or invent an answer. Play the whole app yourself and check every question, answer and explanation against your answer key before students see it.
      
        
      Test it like a studentOpen your link in an Incognito window.
      Open your link in a private (Incognito) window or a student account to make sure it opens without a sign-in. In Apps Script, set "Who has access" to Anyone. If you're signed into several Google accounts, Apps Script can show "page not found," so testing in a private window catches that too.

      Slides that come alive, like this one.
      
    
      More info
      H5P-style activities
      H5P is a popular tool for small interactive activities. AI can build the same kinds of activities as a web app, with no H5P account needed, and you embed them in your lesson.
      Try asking"Make a drag-and-drop where students sort causes and effects of the Silk Road trade."
      Canvas page design
      AI writes the HTML for banners, colored boxes and buttons. Paste it into the HTML editor on any Canvas (LMS) page, so every module looks polished and matches.
      Try asking"Write Canvas-friendly HTML for a Week 3 overview page with a banner, a goals box and a due-dates table."
      Tabbed pages
      Tabs like Overview, Notes, Practice and Check put a full lesson in one place, so students click less and scroll less. Canvas removes some code, so preview the page after you paste it in.
      Try asking"Build a tabbed lesson page for Unit 2 with Overview, Notes, Practice and Check tabs."
      Interactive presentations
      Turn a slide deck into a web page your audience can use: live check-ins, embedded demos, videos and copy buttons, all from one link.
      Try asking"Turn my slides into an interactive web presentation with a live poll on the first page and Next / Previous buttons."
